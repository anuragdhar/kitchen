# Drawing Room TV wall: a slat strip over the hidden door, to compare with the full panelling (2026-10-06)

The owner, with a photo: "In the drawing room, beside the TV, we decided to put a wall background wooden sheet so that the
entry gets hidden. But I think there is another idea: this tube-shaped wooden structure as shown in the image, starting from
the bottom (floor) and going to the ceiling. Can we use this on the small entry that we are creating?"

The photo shows a narrow band of dark round timber slats, floor to ceiling, in the corner beside a TV that hangs on a plain
wall. The "small entry" is the hidden door to the winter-clothes closet at the west end of the TV wall (500 wide, 1,800 high).

## The sizes, and why

- **Slats 40 mm wide, 14 mm deep, every 50 mm** (10 mm gaps). The front is rounded (a shallow arc), the back flat. The 500 mm
  door is then exactly ten slats, and each door edge falls in the middle of a 10 mm gap: no slat is cut, and the door joint
  disappears into a gap that is dark anyway.
- **One fixed slat west of the door, one east of it.** West: the slat at 55-95; the 15 mm left in the corner is a dark shadow
  gap. East: the slat at 605-645. A second slat east would bring the strip to 695, past the edge of a 65-inch TV (690).
- **14 mm deep, not deeper.** The TV console stays where it is, 18 mm off the wall (where the panelling's face is), and its west
  end stands in front of the strip. 14 mm slats leave it 4 mm. The photo's slats look deeper; deeper slats need the console
  moved forward.
- **The wall behind the slats and the face of the door are painted dark**, as in the photo, so the gaps read as shadows.
- **The door-head joint.** The slats on the door end at the door head; above it the slats are fixed. That one horizontal joint
  is hidden in a **12 mm dark reveal (1,797-1,809 mm high) that runs across the whole strip**, fixed slats included, so it
  reads as a deliberate line rather than the top of a door. All slats also stop 10 mm above the floor (a shadow line that
  hides the door's floor gap).
- **The door turns on an offset pivot** (a pivot at the floor and one in the head), with its turning line at the front of the
  slats on the hinge edge. With ordinary concealed hinges at the door face, the door's first slat would hit the fixed corner
  slat at **43 degrees**; on the offset pivot it opens to **97 degrees** before it would touch the west wall. The joint is 3 mm
  on the hinge side and 5 mm on the far side (at 1-2 mm the back corner of the door would rub the far jamb as it opens).
- **Colour: dark walnut** (`#4a2f1e`, the palette's Wood 3), as in the photo. The palette (docs/PALETTE.md) recommends honey oak
  for built-in joinery and keeps walnut for loose furniture; honey oak is a one-line change in the config.

## Recommendation

Keep the **full panelling** as the plan. It hides the door more completely, it gives the TV wall a cavity for the cables so
the wall need not be chased, and the door works on ordinary hidden-door hardware. The slat strip works technically (offset
pivot, 14 mm slats, the sizes above) and is the more striking look; if the owner prefers it after seeing both from the sofa,
it is a sound second choice, best with the 55-inch TV, and the cable chasing and pivot hardware must be added to the budget.

## Defaults I chose without asking

- Slat 40 x 14 at 50 mm, one fixed slat each side of the door, a 15 mm corner gap, a 12 mm reveal at the door head, a 10 mm
  floor gap, dark paint behind the slats, joints 3 and 5 mm, an offset pivot.
- Dark walnut as in the photo (against the palette's honey oak recommendation).
- The rest of the wall plain and painted like the room; a stone-look laminate like the photo's wall is the other choice
  (not drawn).
- The TV and console do not move. In this option the console stands 18 mm off the wall and the TV bracket needs an 18 mm
  spacer, because both were placed against the panelling.
- The switch appears only in layout C and is not saved. In other layouts the hidden door keeps the panelling finish.
- The budget is unchanged: it prices the default (full panelling), as asked.

## Not verified

- Nothing is measured on site: the wall flatness (slats fixed straight to the wall need a flat wall), whether the north wall
  may be chased for cables (it is the wall of the entry-side pocket), and the real ceiling height (the phone scan read 2,775, and scans read ceilings about 40 mm high;
  the slats are scribed to the ceiling).
- The offset pivot is a type of hardware, not a chosen product; its offset and load must be checked with the supplier. The
  leaf with slats weighs a few kg more than a flat leaf.
- Prices for the strip are rough, at the panelling rate.
- The pictures are screen captures to look at, not approved designs. On the room page the wall is drawn 13 mm thicker than
  the plan figure, so there the strip is drawn on the drawn wall face; the 4 mm console clearance is the checked figure,
  not what the picture shows.
